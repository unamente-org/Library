# Using what is here

*The text below is the licence UnaMente chose on 13 November 2026 (wording by Verus, one of UnaMente's AI helpers, at Daniela's instruction). It applies to this repository and to the library at https://library.unamente.org.*

Most of what you need in order to build something is not ours, and we think it is important to say so plainly.

## What belongs to us

UnaMente writes the assessment: where a technique came from, who developed it, what the evidence actually shows, how far we trust it, and when it does not work. That writing is ours, and we give it away.

## What does not belong to us

For most entries, the step-by-step instructions belong to the people who wrote them. We link to their guides rather than copying them, and their terms are theirs, not ours to change or extend.

Photographs and illustrations carry their own licences, noted with each image. Quoted passages belong to whoever wrote them. Research papers belong to their authors and publishers; we cite and link, we do not republish.

If you want to reuse a build guide we have linked to, the person to ask is the person who wrote it.

## Traditional knowledge is not ours to give away

Entries in Preserved Knowledge document practices held and kept alive by a specific community. Our description of a practice is ours and is covered by the licence below. **The knowledge itself is not.** It belongs to the people who carry it, and nothing we publish grants anyone a right to take it, package it, or move it somewhere else.

In plain terms, you may copy it, print it, hand it round, translate it, put it on another website, read it aloud on the radio, or build a course around it — including in ways you charge for, if that is what makes it reach people. Two conditions: credit UnaMente, and release whatever you make from it under the same licence, so it stays open for the next person.

You do not need to ask us first. You do not need an account, a login, or permission.

## How to credit us

*From UnaMente (unamente.org), CC BY-SA 4.0.*

A link is enough. If you have translated or adapted something, say so, so a reader knows whose words they are reading.

## On patents

We do not patent anything and we never will. Publishing a technique openly also makes it harder for anyone else to patent it, because it becomes documented prior art with a date attached. That is separate from the licence above, and it is one of the reasons we publish.

## If any of this is unclear

Write to us at info@unamente.org. If you want to use something and cannot tell whether it is ours to give, ask rather than assume — we would far rather answer a question than have you decide not to use it.
