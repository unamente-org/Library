## What we know

Our Sand Dams entry (trust: Strong) explains how a dam banks water in the sand. In southeast Kenya, researchers visited 97 sand dams in the dry seasons of 2016 and 2017. People were drawing water at only 43% of them. Of the dams with no sign of use, 81% still held water, so a dry dam was not the reason. The study was led by a researcher at Eastern Mennonite University with staff of Utooni Development Organization and SASOL, the two organizations that built most of the dams.

## What we could not find

We searched on 22 November 2026, in English and Kiswahili. We found a 2021 review of sand dams in Kitui County that lists reasons people turn away from a dam, such as salty or bad-tasting water, queues and unclear ownership, and says its authors know of no study of who can reach a dam's water depending on where they live. We found no study that went to dams holding water and asked the people nearby why they did not use them. We could not search in Kikamba.

## What limits it for real

A sand dam only holds water where the riverbed and the rain allow it. Some dams fill with fine silt, and some pumps and wells break. Those are reasons a dam can fail; the question is about dams that did not fail and still went unused.

## What would settle it

Visit dams that hold water but are not used. Talk with the people who live near them: how far is it, how does the water taste, who is allowed to use it, is there a nearer source? Test the water at the same time.
