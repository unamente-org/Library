## What we know

Our Beehive Fences entry (trust: Strong) describes fences of living beehives that turn elephants away from farms. Two long studies have followed them. In Sagalla, Kenya, 23 of 26 fences were still up after as much as nine years, with Save the Elephants, the organization that promotes them, visiting twice a month. In southern Tanzania, a fence kept up by a farmers' group was still working four years after it was built; the Southern Tanzania Elephant Program, which helped build it, was there throughout and did the measuring.

## What we could not find

We searched on 22 November 2026, in English and Kiswahili. We found no study that went back to beehive fences after the organization that helped build them had stopped visiting.

## What limits it for real

Hives only deter elephants when bees live in them, and drought can empty them. Gaps in the wire, or a hive taken down for repair, let elephants through. A fence needs regular upkeep whoever is there.

## What would settle it

Go back to fences some years after the organization that helped build them stopped visiting. Count how many are still standing, how many hives have bees, and whether elephants still get onto the farms. Ask the farmers who keeps the fence up and what it costs them.
