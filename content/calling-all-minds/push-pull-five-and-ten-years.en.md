## What we know

Our Push-Pull entry (trust: Strong) says the biggest obstacle is getting desmodium seed. In Homa Bay County, Kenya, a master's student at Egerton University, with help in collecting the data from staff of ICIPE, the institute that promotes push-pull, found that 74 of 123 farmers who had taken up push-pull were still using it; lack of desmodium seed was the reason most often given by those who had stopped. We could not find in the thesis how long each farmer had been using it. A study led by researchers at ICIPE, with colleagues at Kenya's national agricultural research organization and at Atatürk University, across five countries in East Africa looked at who keeps using push-pull and who stops; we could not read the full paper.

## What we could not find

We searched on 22 November 2026, in English and Kiswahili. We found a study showing that push-pull fields which last keep working well, and a five-country study led by researchers at the institute that promotes push-pull, whose conference poster says many farmers stop within five years but gives no numbers. We found no study that followed plots planted in a known year and counted how many were still in push-pull five and ten years later.

## What limits it for real

Push-pull needs desmodium, and in many places its seed is hard to get. It suits mixed farms that can use the extra fodder.

## What would settle it

Follow plots planted in a known year and check them five and ten years later: are they still in push-pull, and if not, when and why did the farmer stop?
