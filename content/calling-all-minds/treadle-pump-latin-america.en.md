## What we know

Our Treadle Pump entry (trust: Strong) tells how the pump spread in Bangladesh through iDE (International Development Enterprises). A paper by iDE staff says its small irrigation pumps proved successful in parts of Asia, Africa and Latin America, but it names no Latin American country and gives no numbers. iDE has worked in Honduras since 2009, and in Nicaragua. Other pumps worked by hand or foot are already in use in the region, such as Nicaragua's rope pump.

## What we could not find

We searched on 21 and 22 November 2026, in Spanish, Portuguese and English. We found no record of how many treadle pumps were sold in Latin America, where, or whether any are still in use. iDE's own pages for Honduras and Nicaragua describe drip and low-pressure irrigation and do not mention the treadle pump.

## What limits it for real

The same as anywhere: the pump suits small plots with water close to the surface, and it needs a way to buy parts and get repairs.

## What would settle it

Find out where in Latin America treadle pumps were sold, how many, and whether any are still in use. Ask farmers and local makers whether another pump, such as the rope pump, met the same need first.

## How to take part

Tell us you are working on it, send us your results, or point us to a study we missed, through [the Get Involved page](https://www.unamente.org/get-involved). If you worked with iDE in Latin America, we would especially like to hear from you.
