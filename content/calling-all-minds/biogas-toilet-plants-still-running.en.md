## What we know

Our Biogas Digesters entry (trust: Strong) describes community plants that turn toilet waste into cooking gas. What has been measured is for **household** plants, not community ones: in Nepal, researchers from Kathmandu University surveyed 2 559 households with a biogas plant and found 54% of the plants abandoned, mostly because broken parts could not be repaired locally. We have not yet read a study that measured the same thing for community plants linked to toilets.

## What we could not find

We searched on 22 November 2026, in English, Hindi and Nepali. We found a 2011 report on six community and institutional biogas plants in the Kathmandu Valley; it says no agency monitors such plants in Nepal. A 2023 report says India keeps no official count of toilets linked to biogas. We found no survey that counted how many community plants linked to toilets were still running some years after they were built.

## What limits it for real

A digester needs warmth, a steady supply of waste and a way to repair it. Without those it stops, whether it serves one household or a whole settlement.

## What would settle it

Visit community toilet-linked plants built some years ago. Record how many still make gas, who runs and repairs them, and why the others stopped.
