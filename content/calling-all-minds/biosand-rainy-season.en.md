## What we know

Our Biosand Filter entry (trust: Moderate) already asks this. The best trial we found, in homes with and without filters, could not show that the filter protected families in the rainy season, when floods and runoff make water dirtier. Its authors think the reason was something happening in the homes without filters, not a failure of the filter. A second trial, of a plastic biosand filter in Honduras, saw the same pattern.

## What we could not find

We searched on 22 November 2026, in English and Spanish. We found one other trial that reported on the seasons, in Honduras, of a plastic version of the filter. It also found the filter looked more protective in the dry season than in the rains, gave no figures for each season, and had financial support from the organization that made and distributed the filter. We found no trial designed to test any biosand filter in the rainy season or after a flood.

## What limits it for real

A biosand filter does not remove everything. Clean water can get dirty again if it is stored badly, and a filter used too often, left unused for days or stirred too often works less well. Any test has to keep those things the same in every home.

## What would settle it

Measure illness and water quality in the wet season, or after floods, in homes with filters and in comparable homes without them. Check how each family uses and stores its water, and follow them through at least one full rainy season.
